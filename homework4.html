1. Model and Interpretation

An attacker faces n systems one after another. The increment Xᵢ is the penetration score obtained on system i, and the increments are i.i.d. with mean μ and variance σ². The m attackers are m independent copies of the whole trajectory, i.e. the Monte Carlo sample on which every distribution below is estimated.

QuantityMeanVarianceExpected behaviour
Total score Sₙnμnσ²spread grows like √n
Average score Sₙ/nμσ²/n→ μ in probability (LLN): the variance vanishes
Normalized score Sₙ/√nμ√nσ²constant variance; for μ = 0 it converges in distribution to N(0, σ²) (CLT)
  • LLN (in probability): for every ε > 0, P(|Sₙ/n − μ| > ε) → 0. Chebyshev gives the quantitative bound P ≤ σ²/(nε²).
  • CLT: (Sₙ − nμ)/(σ√n) → N(0, 1) in distribution. In the Rademacher case μ = 0 and σ = 1, so this is exactly Sₙ/√n.
  • Bernoulli caveat: since μ = p ≠ 0, the mean of Sₙ/√n is p√n and diverges; the CLT applies to the standardized sum (Sₙ − np)/√(np(1 − p)).

8. Discussion: System Size and Number of Attackers

  • Effect of n (number of systems): the expected total score grows linearly (nμ) and so does its spread, but only like √n. Hence the relative uncertainty σ/(μ√n) shrinks: for a large number of systems the average penetration rate of an attacker is almost deterministic and equal to μ (LLN), while the absolute number of compromised systems still fluctuates by about σ√n. In the symmetric Rademacher case the expected total is 0, but typical excursions are of order √n, so an attacker can be far ahead or behind purely by chance.
  • Effect of m (number of attackers): m does not change the distribution of any single attacker's score; it only improves the estimate of that distribution (sampling errors of order 1/√m, KS critical value about 1.36/√m). If the m attackers are pooled, the aggregate score T is the sum of m independent Sₙ: E[T] = mnμ and Var(T) = mnσ², so the aggregate behaves exactly like a single attacker facing mn systems, and the average per system over all attackers has standard deviation σ/√(mn).
  • Normal approximation: thanks to the CLT, tail probabilities of the total score (for example the chance that an attacker compromises more than a given number of systems) can be approximated by a normal distribution once n is moderately large. For small n and small p the Bernoulli distribution is clearly skewed and the approximation is poor.
  • Limits of the model: real systems are not identically distributed (some are far more vulnerable) and attackers are not independent (they share tools and vulnerabilities). Positive correlation between increments makes Var(Sₙ) grow faster than n and can break the √n scaling.
