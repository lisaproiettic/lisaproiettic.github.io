3. Cryptographic Properties & Discussion

            
    
                
  • Uniformity and Pseudorandomness: Although local changes in $k$ yield coordinate shifts that do not follow a simple linear equation ($y = mx + q$), the outputs are strictly bounded within $\mathbb{Z}_{17}$ and exhibit a repeating cyclic order. This proves that the toy model does not provide true pseudorandomness, but rather a deterministic repeating sequence.
    • 
                
  • Toy vs Real Elliptic Curve: As the Coordinate Distribution Table shows, the scalar multiplication sequence wraps around into a small cyclic group after a few iterations, causing points to repeat. This would lead to a situation where more private keys generate the same public key. In real-world elliptic curves though, like Ethereum's secp256k1, the scale of the group order is exponentially higher, making collisions or accidental key overlaps statistically impossible.
    • 
                
  • Avalanche Effect: Changing $k$ from 6 to 7 (a single step) completely changes the binary representation of both $x$ and $y$. A high bit-distance between adjacent outputs is a core requirement for one-way functions to prevent gradient-based attacks.
